## BUZZ WORDS

### 1. Representation Engineering (RepE)
Top-down control technique that monitors and manipulates high-level internal cognitive states of AI models to regulate safety, truthfulness, and tone without retraining.

### 2. Model Merging (Weight Averaging)
Technique for combining multiple specialized LLMs into a single powerful model by mathematically blending their parameters without additional training overhead.

### 3. Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs)
Interpretability architecture used to untangle millions of overlapping concepts inside neural networks into individual, human-understandable features.

### 4. Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)
A streamlined alternative to RLHF that directly optimizes model parameters using pair-wise human preferences, skipping the need to train a separate reward model.

### 5. Process-Supervised Reward Models (PRMs)
An alignment paradigm that evaluates and scores every intermediate step of a reasoning path—rather than just the final answer—drastically reducing logical hallucination in complex multi-step tasks.

### 6. Dynamic Context Compression
A technique that dynamically compresses long prompt histories into compact state vectors on the fly, allowing LLMs to process million-token contexts with minimal latency and lower memory usage.

### 7. Compound AI Systems
An architectural approach that builds AI applications by orchestrating multiple specialized models, tools, and external datastores together, rather than relying on a single monolithic LLM.

### 8. Continuous Pre-training (Domain Adaptation)
The practice of continuously feeding specialized streaming data to an already trained base model, allowing it to stay updated with new facts without forgetting prior general knowledge.

### 9. Mixture-of-Depths (MoD)
A neural architecture that dynamically allocates different amounts of computation (depth) per token, skipping layers for easy words and diving deep only on complex concepts.

### 10. Semantic Routing
An edge infrastructure technique that uses ultra-lightweight classifiers to dynamically route incoming queries to the most cost-effective model—reserving expensive reasoning tokens only for complex logic.
